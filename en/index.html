FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is Fervon?

A one-person software studio. Jonathan Martín directs fleets of AI agents that build, test and ship; the output is products and open-source tools, not billable hours.

Which products are there, and which are free?

Paid: Trace (local personal memory, $39 one-time) and Veredicto (a CI check against gamed tests, $19 per repository and month). Free and open source: inferbench, Lookspan, ClaudeScope, Mission Control and Pregón.

Can I buy without talking to anyone?

Yes, and that is the normal path. Everything is self-serve: buy or download from the site, with no demo, no sales call and no email-gate.

Does my data leave my machine?

No. The tools run locally and send no telemetry by default. The open-source ones are inspectable on GitHub, so you do not have to take the promise on faith.

Do you take on bespoke development work?

Yes. I apply the same agent engine to your company challenges, per project, with scope and price agreed up front rather than by the hour. The delivered code is yours.

What does "autonomous software" mean?

That execution no longer depends on someone typing: agents build and test in parallel, around the clock. The judgement —what gets built and what gets accepted— stays human.

Who is behind Fervon?

Jonathan Martín, remote from Spain. He is the founder and the studio

What technologies does Fervon use?

TypeScript/Node and React, Python where it fits, SQLite and Postgres, and LLMs both from an API (Claude) and running locally via llama.cpp. Almost everything is designed to run on your machine.

Can I use Fervon tools at my company?

Yes. The open-source tools carry a permissive licence and can be used in commercial projects; the paid products have their own licence, with the price on their page.
